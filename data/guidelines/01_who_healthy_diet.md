# Healthy diet basics (WHO) — summary

Source: World Health Organization. *Healthy diet* fact sheet (2020); *Guideline: Sugars intake for adults and children* (2015); *Guideline: Sodium intake for adults and children* (2012); *Saturated fatty acid and trans-fatty acid intake for adults and children* (2023). Paraphrased summary for a recipe-recommendation assistant; not medical advice.

## Sugars
- Keep **free sugars below 10% of total energy intake**; below 5% (about 25 g, roughly 6 teaspoons, per day for an adult on ~2000 kcal) gives additional benefits.
- Free sugars = sugars added by the manufacturer, cook or consumer, plus sugars naturally present in honey, syrups and fruit juices. Sugars in whole fruit, vegetables and milk are not free sugars.
- Practical cooking tips: reduce sugar in desserts and sauces, prefer whole fruit over juice, avoid sugar-sweetened drinks.

## Salt and sodium
- Adults should eat **less than 5 g of salt per day (about 2 g / 2000 mg sodium)**, and use iodized salt.
- Most salt comes from processed foods (bread, cured meats, cheese, sauces, stock cubes, instant noodles) rather than the salt shaker.
- Cooking tips: season with herbs, spices, citrus and vinegar; choose low-sodium stock and canned goods; rinse canned beans.
- WHO also recommends at least 3.5 g (3510 mg) of potassium per day from foods such as vegetables, fruit, beans and nuts.

## Fats
- Total fat below 30% of energy; **saturated fat at most 10%** and trans fat at most 1% of energy.
- Replace saturated fats (fatty meat, butter, cream, cheese, coconut and palm oil) with unsaturated fats (fish, avocado, nuts, olive/canola/sunflower oil).

## Fruit, vegetables and fibre
- Eat at least 400 g (five portions) of fruit and vegetables per day; include whole grains and legumes.
