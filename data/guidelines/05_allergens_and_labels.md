# Food allergens and nutrition labels — summary

Sources: US Food Allergen Labeling and Consumer Protection Act (FALCPA, 2004) and FASTER Act (2021); Gupta R.S. et al., *Prevalence and severity of food allergies among US adults*, JAMA Network Open 2(1), 2019; UK Department of Health / FSA, *Guide to creating a front of pack (FoP) nutrition label* (2016). Paraphrased summary; not medical advice.

## The nine major allergens (US)
Milk, eggs, fish, crustacean shellfish, tree nuts, peanuts, wheat, soybeans and — since 1 January 2023 — sesame. About 10.8% of US adults have a convincing food allergy; shellfish is the most common, followed by milk, peanut and tree nuts.

## Safety rules for recipe suggestions
- An allergen must be avoided completely; "a little" is not safe. Check every ingredient, including hidden sources: Worcestershire sauce and Caesar dressing (anchovy/fish), pesto (nuts, cheese), soy sauce (soy and wheat), mayonnaise (egg), ghee and whey (milk), tahini (sesame), marzipan and praline (tree nuts).
- Cross-contact in the kitchen (shared boards, oil, fryers) can cause reactions; clean utensils and surfaces.
- Substitutions must remove the allergen entirely (e.g., oat or soy milk for milk allergy, but soy milk is not suitable for a soy allergy).
- Anyone with symptoms of a severe reaction (breathing difficulty, swelling) needs emergency care.

## Reading nutrition levels (UK front-of-pack thresholds)
Per portion, a food is **high** (red) when it has more than: 21 g fat, 6 g saturated fat, 27 g total sugars, or 1.8 g salt (about 720 mg sodium). Per 100 g, the high thresholds are 17.5 g fat, 5 g saturated fat, 22.5 g sugars and 1.5 g salt. Low (green) per 100 g: at most 3 g fat, 1.5 g saturated fat, 5 g sugars, 0.3 g salt.
