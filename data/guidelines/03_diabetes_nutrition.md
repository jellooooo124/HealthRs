# Nutrition for type 2 diabetes (ADA) — summary

Source: American Diabetes Association. *Standards of Care in Diabetes* — Facilitating Positive Health Behaviors (nutrition therapy section); ADA consumer guidance on carbohydrate counting and the Diabetes Plate Method. Paraphrased summary; not medical advice.

## Principles
- There is **no single ideal split** of carbohydrate, protein and fat for everyone; eating plans should be individualized with a registered dietitian.
- Emphasize non-starchy vegetables, minimize **added sugars and refined grains**, and choose whole, minimally processed foods.
- Fibre: at least 14 g per 1000 kcal; whole grains, legumes, vegetables and fruit help.
- Replace sugar-sweetened beverages (including fruit juice) with water or unsweetened drinks.
- People on fixed insulin doses benefit from a consistent amount of carbohydrate at meals; changes to insulin or other medication must be made with their care team.

## Carbohydrate counting and the plate method
- A common starting point is about **45–60 g of carbohydrate per meal**, adjusted to the person's needs (our system flags a serving above 60 g).
- Diabetes Plate Method (9-inch plate): **half non-starchy vegetables, a quarter lean protein, a quarter carbohydrate foods**, with water or a zero-calorie drink.
- Sugars: desserts and sweet sauces add quickly; our system flags a serving with more than 27 g total sugars (UK front-of-pack "high" threshold per portion).

## Cooking tips
- Swap white rice/pasta/bread for whole-grain versions or cauliflower/zucchini alternatives; add beans and lentils.
- Use spices, vanilla or cinnamon instead of extra sugar; reduce sugar in baking by about a third.
- Pair carbohydrate foods with protein, fibre and healthy fats to slow glucose rise.
