# Diet for gout and high uric acid (ACR 2020) — summary

Source: FitzGerald J.D. et al. *2020 American College of Rheumatology Guideline for the Management of Gout.* Arthritis Care & Research 72(6), 2020. Paraphrased summary; not medical advice.

## Lifestyle recommendations (all conditional, low-certainty evidence)
- **Limit alcohol intake** — beer and spirits are most strongly associated with gout flares.
- **Limit purine intake** — especially organ meats and certain seafood.
- **Limit high-fructose corn syrup** (sweetened drinks, many sweets and sauces).
- Weight loss for people with overweight; the guideline advises **against** taking vitamin C supplements for gout.
- Diet alone rarely controls hyperuricemia; urate-lowering medication decisions belong to the doctor.

## Foods to limit (high purine)
- Organ meats: liver, kidney, sweetbreads, tripe, heart, brains, giblets.
- Seafood: anchovies, sardines, herring, mackerel, mussels, scallops, clams, oysters, shrimp, crab, lobster.
- Game meats (venison, goose); yeast extracts (Marmite, Vegemite).
- Large portions of red meat.

## Generally suitable choices
- Vegetables (including purine-containing vegetables such as spinach and mushrooms, which are not linked to more flares), fruit, whole grains, legumes, eggs, poultry in moderate portions, low-fat dairy, plenty of water.

## How our system applies this
- A recipe is flagged for hyperuricemia if its ingredients or steps mention a high-purine food, alcohol (beer, spirits, liqueur) or corn syrup, or if a serving has more than 27 g total sugars.
