# Diet for high blood pressure (ACC/AHA 2017, DASH) — summary

Source: Whelton P.K. et al. *2017 ACC/AHA Guideline for the Prevention, Detection, Evaluation, and Management of High Blood Pressure in Adults.* Hypertension 71(6), 2018; DASH (Dietary Approaches to Stop Hypertension) eating plan, US NHLBI. Paraphrased summary; not medical advice.

## Key non-drug measures recommended for adults with elevated blood pressure or hypertension
- **Sodium reduction:** the optimal goal is **below 1500 mg sodium per day**; for most adults, aim for at least a 1000 mg/day reduction from current intake. Sodium reduction lowers systolic blood pressure by roughly 5–6 mm Hg in people with hypertension.
- **DASH diet:** rich in vegetables, fruits, whole grains and low-fat dairy, with reduced saturated and total fat. In people with hypertension DASH lowers systolic pressure by about 11 mm Hg.
- **Potassium:** increase dietary potassium to about 3500–5000 mg/day, preferably from food (vegetables, fruit, beans), unless the person has kidney disease or takes medicines that raise potassium — that must be checked with a doctor.
- **Weight loss** for people with overweight (about 1 mm Hg per kg lost), regular physical activity.
- **Alcohol:** men no more than 2 standard drinks per day, women no more than 1.

## Translating this into recipes
- Per main meal, about one third of the daily sodium goal: roughly **500 mg sodium per serving** (the threshold our system uses).
- High-sodium ingredients to limit: soy sauce, fish sauce, bouillon/stock cubes, cured and processed meats (bacon, ham, sausage), cheese, pickles, canned soups, seasoning packets.
- Saturated fat: keep a serving at or below about 6 g (UK front-of-pack "high" threshold per portion); prefer lean meat, poultry without skin, fish, beans.
- Flavour without salt: garlic, onion, ginger, herbs, pepper, citrus juice and zest, vinegar.
